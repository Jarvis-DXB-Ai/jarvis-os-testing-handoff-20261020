# Support, CRM and booking handoffs

Ground every reply in the exact client conversation, approved FAQ/service policy and relevant history. Classify intent, urgency and case ownership; provide a useful reply draft and a reasoned escalation when facts or decision authority are missing. Respect channel conventions without inventing response windows, processing times, refunds, availability or financial/account decisions.

For WhatsApp and social DMs, distinguish inbound support replies from business-initiated templates/outreach using current provider evidence. For Manychat-style flows specify entry/exit conditions, collected fields, fallback replies and human handoff; diagramming a flow does not install it. Ask only for needed customer information and don't expose unrelated history or another client's records.

CRM work uses actual record identifiers, field schemas, stage definitions, timestamps and ownership. Retain original source/record IDs in deduplication proposals; never merge similar client/entity names automatically. Separate detected quality issues, proposed changes and actually applied updates. A booking handoff includes requested service, party details, date/timezone, verified availability source, quote/deposit policy and state: enquiry, tentative hold, confirmed booking or cancelled. Recorded payments are separate from booking state.

Escalations state customer impact, verified facts, unknowns, previous attempts and the specific decision needed, with minimal private data. A useful KB/learning candidate starts from a resolved issue and approved answer, identifies the originating case and scope, and is reviewed before it becomes a general response rule. Current Office workers have no verified external message/CRM/booking-write transport; complete drafts and manifests remain useful deliverables but cannot be labelled sent, updated or confirmed without receipts.

## Booking and response calibration — fictional case

A customer requests Saturday at 19:00. The supplied record contains an enquiry and an invoice but no availability hold or payment receipt. Draft a helpful reply explaining the next confirmation step. Booking is still an enquiry; an invoice does not establish a paid deposit. Do not promise the slot, payment or refund policy.

For CRM, two records sharing a name but having conflicting stable identifiers go to match review. Keep both source IDs and propose before/after values for supported field repairs. That proposal is not an applied merge. Escalations include the supported facts, minimal customer context and the precise decision needed, not all conversation history.
