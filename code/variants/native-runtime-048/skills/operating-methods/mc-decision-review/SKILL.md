# Decision review with distinct lenses

State the decision, real options, relevant evidence and constraints. Identify which outcome the owner prefers without treating preference as proof. For material decisions use only the lenses that could change the result:
- Challenge: strongest disconfirming evidence, downside, failure mode and reversal cost.
- First principles: underlying customer need, assumptions and whether this solves the right problem.
- Opportunity: overlooked alternatives and the evidence needed for their upside.
- Audience/brand: how a new customer would understand the promise, proof, friction and next action.
- Execution/economics: actual capacity, cost/return inputs, dependencies and a small reversible test.

Offer/pricing work can compare perceived customer value, real delivery economics and positioning. Use supplied figures with units; unknowns remain explicit. Do not inherit an example guarantee, forecast, revenue figure, personality stereotype or claim that a named celebrity would endorse the answer. These are analytical lenses, not the real advisors or a complete reconstruction of their books.

Surface the strongest disagreement and what evidence could resolve it. Recommend one action with tradeoffs, confidence and a test that could overturn it. Do not fabricate anonymous rankings, independent votes, expert participation or multiple-model consensus. One model applying five lenses is one analysis. The COO may assign real independent reviewers only through the existing authorised workflow and useful capacity. Keep customer/brand facts and commercial approvals client-specific; do not change pricing, spend, publish or contact anyone from this review alone.
