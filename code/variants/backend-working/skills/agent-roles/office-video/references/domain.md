# Reference-led photography, video and editing

Separate the **subject source**, **style reference**, **approved logo**, **generated concept** and **finished output**. Product dimensions, venue geometry, faces/identities and supplied artwork are constraints; aesthetic examples establish lighting/composition, not factual structure. A food studio image, drink studio image and product lifestyle image are different asset families in the existing Brain; use exact source IDs and revisions rather than blending their contexts.

A photo brief specifies subject/action, scene purpose, framing, lighting direction/quality, material response, lens perspective/depth, crop/safe space, required references and intended use. Describe what should exist physically; avoid contradictory light/camera instructions. A video plan adds timeline, action/motion, camera movement, continuity dependencies, transitions, audio/voice, text, rights and final format. Check summed scene duration and narrative payoff. Do not force every project into an upstream fixed clip length or model preset.

Choose a generation/edit workflow only from the run's verified tools. The current Office `graphic_card` is a simple SVG/PNG renderer, `blender_scene` creates a primitive draft, and `video_from_card` makes a silent still-based MP4: none of these produces a realistic photoshoot or edited live footage. Without the relevant route, finish the usable shot brief, prompts, narration or edit decision list and state the blocked production step. Do not invoke bootstrap installers, pay for jobs or claim a render on the strength of a skill file.

Produced media requires an asset manifest: source IDs, settings/model receipt when available, output IDs, intended crop/format, rights, review version and known deviations. Reference fidelity, identity continuity, logos, anatomy, text, audio sync and actual visual quality require pixels/audio and rendered review. Metadata can establish file existence/duration/codec; it cannot establish composition quality. Unviewed/unheard checks remain unassessed. Give repair instructions tied to frames/timecodes and exact source references.

## Shot calibration — fictional example

A drink source shows a clear glass with a fixed label and garnish. Separate the locked glass/mark/garnish source from a reference for side lighting. Specify the shot as a three-quarter close view, soft side key, controlled reflections, legible label and crop space for the requested layout. Do not turn a style reference's bottle into the subject. If accurate lettering cannot be generated, request approved artwork compositing through an available production route.

For a requested 12-second sequence, 0–3, 3–8 and 8–12 seconds total 12 seconds. That proves storyboard timing only. Clip existence, actual duration, transitions and audio still require output evidence. A reference frame cannot establish the full video's continuity.
